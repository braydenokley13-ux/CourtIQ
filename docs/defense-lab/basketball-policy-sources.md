# Basketball policy research and bounded attack contract

Checked 5 October 2026. These are practitioner explanations and clinic material, not controlled studies or validation of the engine's timing constants. I fetched public article text through source pages or publisher WordPress endpoints. The summaries below state only the basketball ideas needed to constrain this vertical slice.

## Structures, language and preferences

| Structure to represent | Language found in coaching material | What varies by program |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ball-screen pair must account for handler and screener | high/flat/at-level/drop; show/hedge; blitz/trap; switch; ICE/down/blue | exact depth, direction, duration, who calls it, and whether the rule changes by side/personnel. Similar labels are not guaranteed synonyms. ICE is ordinarily a side-screen force rule; applying it to a middle screen needs an explicit approximation. |
| A defender away from the screen can momentarily help on the roll or drive | tag, bump, stunt, low man, early help | eligible helper, commit depth, duration and shooter protection. “Tag” may mean a body bump or sustained contact depending on the staff. |
| Remaining defenders split or exchange multiple threats after help | split, take two, X-out, rotate, help the helper | which shot/receiver is conceded, when the exchange starts, and whether the exchange is two-player or a longer chain. An X-out is an exchange pattern, not one universal assignment map. |
| A defender can transfer responsibility under a distinct trigger | screen switch, peel, scram | Called screen switch follows the screen rule; peel can be a beaten on-ball defender exchanging out while a nearby helper contains; scram can correct a mismatch away from the ball. These should share transfer mechanics, not be synonyms or one trigger. |
| Offense changes screen or receiver geometry | reject, re-screen, slip/ghost, short roll, pop, lift, drift, shake, skip, one-more | timing, angle, screening contact/commitment, who reads the next advantage and the intended receiver. A ghost is a screen fake/quick release; it cannot be represented as a stationary screen with a different shot modifier. |

“Universal” here means the structural relation needed by a coherent model, not a mandatory coaching choice. The structural invariants are paired ball/screener tasks; finite movement and ball travel; help temporarily exposing another threat; and responsibility transfer having a trigger and a later physical arrival. Names, thresholds, preferred concessions and rotation timing remain team preferences. The well-established concepts still have disagreement in terminology and use. Never infer one team's exact rule from a label alone.

- [Basketball Immersion, Observations from the 2019 U18 European Basketball Championships](https://basketballimmersion.com/european-basketball-championships/), public post 10411: discusses Spain/Stack guard-big transfer alternatives, middle-screen tag/skip, empty-corner low-tag geometry and short-roll reads. - [Basketball Immersion, Mastering Peel Switching](https://basketballimmersion.com/mastering-peel-switching-in-basketball/), public post: its retrieved text teaches “beat → peel → switch,” nearest-player contain, the beaten defender leaving the ball, and adapting the rule for a less mobile big. Implication: a bounded first slice should not globally optimize all matchups; team personnel and influence direction matter.
- [Basketball Immersion, Masterclass with Dom Laviolette on Spain Pick and Roll](https://basketballimmersion.com/masterclass-with-dom-laviolette-on-spain-pick-and-roll/), source page inspected: Spain is a named teaching topic, but accessible source text did not provide enough freely readable tactical detail to support additional fine-grained rules. The structural three-player back-screen dependency is therefore treated as an architecture test, not as an extracted doctrine.
- [Coach's Clipboard, 2–3 Zone Defense](https://www.coachesclipboard.net/23zonedefense.html): retrieved article describes on/off bump exchanges, skip responsibility, corner traps and high-post duties. Implication: future ontology must permit area/threat ownership; a five-player one-to-one permutation is not a universal defensive model.
- [USA Basketball, Youth Basketball Guidelines](https://www.usab.com/youth/development/youth-basketball-guidelines): retrieved age-specific development guidance. It is not evidence for varsity scheme thresholds. Implication: do not mistake developmental recommendations for high-school tactical law.

An additional publicly readable [Basketball Immersion peel-switch article](https://basketballimmersion.com/mastering-peel-switching-in-basketball/) describes the immediate exchange cue, next defender containing the ball, and adapting a peel scheme for a slower big. This is one coach's teaching explanation, not proof the technique is universally preferred. A public article titled [Mastering the Hostage Dribble](https://basketballimmersion.com/mastering-the-hostage-dribble/) presents the dribble as ball protection against a drop. I use it only to support the possibility that an offense can manipulate the handler's hold/read window; it is a skill cue, not a distinct offense policy to simulate.

## Bounded offense mutations: basketball validity

The strategy knobs describe bounded changes to the offense's intent; they are not learned tendencies or success probabilities. Mutations apply to targets/reads before movement is advanced, preserve player velocity, and are clamped to authored legal court points. A strategy-only test should not rewrite events that already happened.

| Mutation | Basketball trigger/meaning | Bounded interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Screen angle | Screen is set on a finite approach; handler may use/reject its side. | Offset the authored screen approach within ±0.65 rad. Do not teleport the screener or reverse a launched pass. |
| Lift delay / width | Weak-side receiver relocates after the screen/roll draws attention. Teams use lift, shake, drift or stay based on drive line and spacing. | Alter one receiver's start/read target by a small bounded time/space offset; require the lift lane to remain in bounds and preserve player acceleration. Not a global formation warp. |
| Reject | Handler attacks away from the screen when defender overplays/leans to it. | Activate only if the handler still controls the ball and the observed screen-side relation supports rejection. It should change drive/contain geometry, not guarantee a score. |
| Re-screen | First angle fails or handler does not gain an advantage; screener changes angle and screens again. | Permit once per possession, after the first screen interaction and before the read/clock cutoff; require spacing and a new approach. Prevent infinite loops. |
| Short roll | Big releases early into space when the defense commits/hedges/blitzes; becomes receiver and makes the next read. | Trigger from observed ball pressure/coverage geometry; read available corner/lift/drive after catch. Do not force the pass or claim the receiver sees all future defense. |
| Pop | Screener opens away from basket to punish a big who protects the paint. | Replace roll target with a legal perimeter opening; availability depends on pass path and current closeout/reach geometry. |
| Slip / ghost | Screener leaves before establishing a full stationary screen, often attacking a defender's anticipation. | Model a distinct early release route and avoid assuming contact. It is not merely an ordinary roll with a different name. |
| Spain / back screen | Third attacker back-screens the screener's defender during a ball screen, adding a new 3-player interaction. | Architecture/content extension. Do not claim the 2-player P&R template simulates the back screen or its switches. |
| Empty-side P&R | No weak-side corner receiver; weak-side low defender can be pulled into a two-on-one on the remaining side. | Derive eligible helper and pass geometry from actual spacing. Do not reuse a fixed weak-corner role. |
| Skip / one-more | A long pass and subsequent pass move the defense through a chain of closeouts. | Use separate finite ball-flight segments and read the next receiver only after catch. A skip is not instantaneous; the one-more can expose a defender reversing direction. |

Numerical offsets and speed/reaction assumptions are engineering bounds chosen for safe experiments; no cited clinic establishes them as human averages. Expose them as bounded opponent settings and disclose the assumptions. Tests can establish deterministic causal behavior and geometric validity, not tactical effectiveness in live games.

## Integration decisions

- Optional `opponent` input keeps old saved answer JSON valid and preserves its exact legacy behavior. New runs receive an explicit strategy with reject/re-screen/short-roll enabled and zero offsets.
- Timestamped opponent edits are append-only experiment inputs with the same chronological, duration and unique-ID checks as defensive edits. A change takes effect at its tick; already-released ball flight and prior history remain fixed.
- Tags, pop, peel, X-out and screen switch are not interchangeable enum labels. Model their triggers and responsibilities explicitly as the content graph grows.
- Alias vocabulary belongs in coach-facing terminology, not the physics policy. “ICE”, “blue”, “down”, “show” and “hedge” require a team-specific mapping before the engine can claim equivalence.
- Report computed geometry and timing under named assumptions. Do not translate source terminology or bounded model outputs into shooting percentages, defensive grades, or win predictions.
